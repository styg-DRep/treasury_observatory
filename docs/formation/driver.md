# Treasury Observatory — Driver

## Normalized Driver

Public treasury activity generates substantial evidence about costs, estimates, delivery, outcomes, and the behavior of the funding system, but that evidence remains distributed and difficult to compare or carry forward across funding decisions and governance cycles.

Consequently, participants repeatedly make stewardship decisions without a durable shared capacity to learn from prior activity, recognize emerging systemic patterns, or distinguish warranted uncertainty from avoidable information asymmetry.

Strengthening that capacity could help the funding commons adapt its practices while preserving plural judgment and decentralized decision-making.

> **Why this Driver uses non-evaluative language**
>
> A Driver describes a situation that gives people reason to respond. It does not need to determine in advance whether that situation is good or bad, nor what response should follow.
>
> Terms such as *excessive*, *inefficient*, *inflated*, *wasteful*, *insufficient*, or *unfair* can quietly embed conclusions that the evidence may not yet support. In a decentralized funding commons, that matters especially because different participants may reasonably interpret the same conditions differently.
>
> The Driver therefore favors observable conditions and consequences over judgments about them. Where interpretation is necessary, uncertainty is made explicit.
>
> This is not only a writing convention. It reflects an epistemic commitment: **keep observation, interpretation, and judgment distinguishable for as long as doing so remains useful.**

## Current Conditions

A decentralized public treasury distributes shared resources through many separate funding decisions made by participants with differing expertise, incentives, information, and governance responsibilities.

Information relevant to those decisions exists across proposals, budgets, governance discussions, milestone reports, audits, transaction records, delivery artifacts, market information, and prior funding histories.

This information is often:

- expressed differently across proposals and funding processes;
- difficult to compare across projects or governance cycles;
- oriented toward individual funding decisions rather than longitudinal learning;
- separated from later evidence about delivery, expenditure, or outcomes;
- interpreted by different participants without a durable shared record of those interpretations;
- only partially connected to external reference data or comparable activity.

Innovative and specialized work further complicates comparison because useful reference classes may be incomplete, rapidly changing, or absent.

---

## Current and Anticipated Effects

Participants frequently reconstruct context and pricing judgments when evaluating new proposals.

This can make it difficult to distinguish among:

- market movement and proposal-specific pricing;
- novel work and work that is difficult to specify;
- uncertainty inherent in the work and uncertainty introduced by estimation;
- ecosystem-wide conditions and project-specific conditions;
- isolated observations and recurring patterns.

Historical treasury activity therefore generates experience that does not always become equally accessible shared learning.

Over time, patterns in estimation, funding concentration, administrative burden, delivery variance, pricing, or other treasury effects may emerge without becoming readily visible across the ecosystem.

Responses intended to improve visibility can themselves influence behavior. Published benchmarks, standardized methods, review processes, or evaluator roles may affect proposal design, pricing, participation, and governance judgment.

---

## Relevance to the Funding Commons

A public treasury depends not only on mechanisms for allocating resources, but also on participants' ability to learn from previous allocations and carry that learning into future decisions.

Greater shared capacity to observe, compare, interpret, and learn from treasury activity could support:

- more informed cost and funding judgments;
- clearer representation of uncertainty;
- accumulation of historical experience into accessible institutional memory;
- earlier recognition of recurring or emerging patterns;
- comparison of competing interpretations against shared evidence;
- adaptation of treasury practices as conditions change;
- continued use of distributed rather than singular judgment.

The underlying need is therefore broader than cost verification.

It is a need for **durable, shared, contestable capacity for learning about the behavior of the funding commons itself**.

---

## Driver Boundary

This Driver does not presume:

- that treasury prices are systematically high or low;
- that historical prices represent appropriate future prices;
- that a single cost model can apply across all forms of treasury-funded activity;
- that an independent evaluator should determine whether proposals are correctly priced;
- that greater standardization is always preferable;
- that the appropriate response must take the form of a Treasury Observatory.

These remain matters for inquiry, experimentation, and design.

The working concept of a **Treasury Observatory** is one possible response to this Driver.
