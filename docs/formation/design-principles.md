# Treasury Observatory — Design Principles

## Status

**Formation-stage design guidance**

These principles describe how the Treasury Observatory should seek to hold its stewardship domain.

They are not implementation requirements, constitutional law, or a finished governance framework.

Their purpose is to provide criteria against which emerging structures, methods, practices, and experiments can be examined.

Where principles come into tension, the tension should be made visible rather than hidden through premature prioritization.

---

# 1. Steward the Conditions for Judgment, Not Judgment Itself

The Observatory should improve the conditions under which treasury participants can make judgments without attempting to substitute its own judgment for theirs.

This means improving:

- access to evidence;
- comparability;
- provenance;
- uncertainty representation;
- historical memory;
- methodological transparency;
- opportunities for competing interpretation.

It should avoid converting analytical capacity into decision authority.

### Design test

**Does this mechanism help participants make better-informed judgments, or does it begin deciding on their behalf?**

---

# 2. Keep Observation, Interpretation, and Judgment Distinguishable

Observations, analytical transformations, interpretations, and governance judgments are different kinds of claims.

The Observatory should preserve those distinctions wherever useful.

For example:

**Observation:**  
A proposal requests $190/hour for a category of engineering labor.

**Interpretation:**  
That rate is higher than the median of a selected reference class.

**Hypothesis:**  
The difference may reflect scarcity of relevant expertise.

**Judgment:**  
The treasury should fund the proposal.

Collapsing these into a single rating conceals important reasoning.

### Design test

**Can a participant tell what was observed, what was inferred, what remains hypothetical, and where judgment entered?**

---

# 3. Make Uncertainty Visible

Uncertainty is information.

The Observatory should not reward apparent precision where the underlying domain is uncertain.

Useful outputs may therefore include:

- ranges;
- confidence intervals;
- scenario bounds;
- reference-class strength;
- known unknowns;
- assumptions;
- sensitivity to key variables.

Novel activity may legitimately produce high uncertainty even when its estimate is carefully constructed.

### Design test

**Does this representation communicate the actual state of knowledge, or merely make the result look more certain?**

---

# 4. Centralize Memory More Readily Than Authority

Some forms of centralization may be useful.

A shared registry, historical dataset, method library, or schema can increase collective learning.

The same does not automatically justify centralized interpretive or decision authority.

The Observatory should therefore be more willing to concentrate:

- records;
- provenance;
- shared standards;
- version histories;
- common infrastructure;

than to concentrate:

- interpretation;
- accreditation;
- agenda-setting;
- proposal approval;
- funding authority.

### Design test

**What is being centralized here: memory and interoperability, or authority and judgment?**

---

# 5. Authority Should Be Bounded, Explicit, and Temporary Where Possible

Distributed systems still require authority.

The relevant question is not whether authority exists, but how it is constituted.

Authority should be:

- attached to a defined responsibility;
- visible to participants;
- limited in scope;
- reviewable;
- reversible where practical;
- time-bounded when the function itself is temporary.

A temporary inquiry may require a facilitator. A data schema may require a steward. Neither role needs authority beyond the work being held.

### Design test

**Can we identify exactly what this actor is authorized to decide, for whom, under what conditions, and for how long?**

---

# 6. Accountability Should Follow Effects, Not Only Roles

Formal role descriptions are insufficient in a distributed commons.

Influence may emerge through:

- widely used analytical models;
- trusted dashboards;
- control over important datasets;
- recurring facilitation roles;
- reputation;
- funding relationships;
- technical infrastructure.

The Observatory should therefore pay attention to both formal authority and de facto influence.

### Design test

**Who can materially affect outcomes through this mechanism, whether or not they hold a formal role?**

---

# 7. Shared Evidence Does Not Require Shared Interpretation

The Observatory should seek common evidentiary ground without requiring interpretive consensus.

Different participants may reasonably reach different conclusions from the same observations.

Where interpretations diverge, the system should make visible:

- the evidence each interpretation relies on;
- relevant assumptions;
- methodological differences;
- unresolved uncertainty.

Pluralism is not a failure of analysis.

### Design test

**Does this mechanism allow disagreement to become more legible, or does it force disagreement into a single score or official conclusion?**

---

# 8. Methods Should Be Inspectable, Versioned, and Contestable

An analytical method is itself a claim about how evidence should be transformed.

Methods should therefore expose:

- assumptions;
- data requirements;
- transformations;
- limitations;
- applicable contexts;
- version history.

Methods may improve, compete, or become obsolete.

No method should acquire legitimacy solely through institutional adoption.

### Design test

**Could another participant understand, reproduce, challenge, or improve the method used here?**

---

# 9. Credibility Should Be Earned Empirically and Contextually

The Observatory should avoid treating credentials, office, or reputation as sufficient proxies for analytical reliability.

Where possible, credibility should emerge from evidence such as:

- accuracy of previous estimates;
- calibration of uncertainty claims;
- reproducibility;
- quality of provenance;
- responsiveness to error;
- domain-specific track record.

Credibility may be contextual.

Someone well calibrated in infrastructure costing may have little demonstrated reliability in protocol research estimation.

### Design test

**What evidence supports trusting this contribution in this particular context?**

---

# 10. Historical Evidence Should Inform Without Becoming Normative by Default

Historical treasury records can support reference classes, calibration, trend detection, and inquiry.

They should not automatically establish what future work ought to cost or how future funding ought to behave.

Historical patterns may themselves reflect:

- changing market conditions;
- earlier information asymmetries;
- path dependence;
- treasury-specific incentives;
- previous governance structures.

History should therefore function primarily as evidence for inquiry.

### Design test

**Are historical observations being used to illuminate the present, or silently converted into rules for the future?**

---

# 11. Preserve the Possibility of Novelty

A treasury that funds innovation will encounter work for which strong comparables do not exist.

The Observatory should distinguish low comparability from poor quality.

Novel proposals may require different forms of assurance:

- explicit assumptions;
- scenario modeling;
- staged experimentation;
- milestone learning;
- smaller reversible commitments.

Novelty should increase inquiry, not automatically increase suspicion.

### Design test

**Does this design allow the system to say “we do not yet know” without translating that into “therefore reject”?**

---

# 12. Prefer Reversible Experiments to Premature Institutionalization

During formation, uncertainty about the Observatory itself should be addressed through bounded experiments where possible.

Examples include:

- testing a cost schema on historical proposals;
- comparing independent estimation methods;
- running a temporary inquiry;
- publishing an experimental reference class;
- evaluating how DReps actually use a new analytical artifact.

Structures should become durable only when experience provides reason for durability.

### Design test

**Could we learn what we need to know through a smaller, reversible intervention first?**

---

# 13. Let Structure Follow Recurring Need

Roles, committees, schemas, and processes should emerge in response to demonstrated recurring conditions.

The Observatory should resist building organizational machinery merely because such machinery is conventional.

A recurring stewardship need may justify a durable role.

A temporary question may justify only a temporary inquiry.

### Design test

**What recurring condition makes this permanent structure necessary?**

---

# 14. Keep Participation Costs Proportionate

Transparency and assurance are not free.

Every reporting requirement, data field, review process, or methodological standard creates costs for participants.

The value of additional information should therefore be considered alongside:

- proposer burden;
- contributor burden;
- opportunity cost;
- barriers to participation;
- effects on smaller or less-resourced actors.

### Design test

**Is the additional learning generated by this requirement proportionate to the burden it creates?**

---

# 15. Treat the Observatory as Part of the System It Observes

The Observatory will alter the treasury commons simply by existing.

Its methods may influence:

- pricing;
- proposal structure;
- governance attention;
- participant behavior;
- funding strategy;
- perceptions of legitimacy.

These effects should be observable and open to inquiry.

The Observatory should not assume neutrality merely because its role is informational.

### Design test

**What behavior might change because this mechanism exists, and how would we notice?**

---

# 16. Preserve Reflexivity as a First-Class Function

The Observatory should periodically examine its own assumptions, structures, incentives, and effects.

Reflexive inquiry may ask:

- Which methods have become dominant?
- Which participants are being heard or excluded?
- Which measures are being optimized against?
- Where has informal authority accumulated?
- What burdens have emerged?
- Which outputs are actually being used?
- Where has the original Driver changed?

Reflexivity should not be treated as occasional institutional housekeeping.

It is part of the Observatory's developmental function.

### Design test

**Does the system contain a credible way to learn when its own design has become part of the problem?**

---

# 17. Optimize for Learning Across Time, Not Merely Decisions in the Moment

A funding decision produces more than an allocation outcome.

It can also produce evidence.

The Observatory should therefore help connect:

**estimate → decision → implementation → actuals → outcome → interpretation → future inquiry**

This temporal integration allows individual observations to become trajectories.

Trajectories may reveal:

- direction of change;
- rate of change;
- persistence;
- convergence or divergence;
- structural breaks;
- recurring intervention effects.

This can support governance judgment that is less dependent on isolated present-state snapshots.

A system that improves one decision while forgetting what happened afterward sacrifices much of the long-term value of observation.

### Design test

**What will this decision teach future participants, how will that learning remain accessible, and what trajectory might become visible over time?**

---

# 18. Follow Consequential Uncertainty

Formation work should preferentially investigate uncertainties whose resolution could materially alter the Observatory's direction, structure, methods, or justification.

Not all unknowns deserve equal attention.

Where several possible next actions exist, preference should be given to work that combines:

- high consequence if the underlying assumption is wrong;
- meaningful testability under current conditions;
- influence on other important hypotheses;
- potential to generate learning across several design questions.

This principle does not imply that easily measured questions are more important than difficult ones.

Some highly consequential hypotheses may require long periods of observation. They should remain visible even when they cannot yet be tested directly.

The purpose is to choose the most informative available next step, not to optimize for activity.

### Design test

**What consequential uncertainty will this work reduce, and what might we do differently depending on what we learn?**

---

# 19. Favor Commons Capacity Over Institutional Dependency

A successful Observatory should increase the ecosystem's distributed ability to reason about treasury activity.

It should not make good judgment increasingly dependent on a small group of specialists or on the continued existence of one institution.

Useful methods, data, and practices should therefore remain transferable and reusable.

### Design test

**If the Observatory disappeared, would the commons retain more evaluative capacity than it had before?**

---

# 20. Preserve Separation Between Cost, Delivery, and Public Value

These are related but distinct domains.

**Cost confidence** asks whether an estimate is well supported.

**Delivery confidence** asks whether the proposed work is likely to be carried out as intended.

**Public value** asks whether the outcome is worth pursuing with shared resources.

None should silently substitute for another.

### Design test

**Is this analytical output answering the question it claims to answer, or smuggling in a different judgment?**

---

# 21. Prefer Generative Constraints to Comprehensive Rules

The Observatory should seek a small number of principles that improve behavior across many contexts rather than attempting to prescribe every possible situation.

Rules may be necessary where interoperability or accountability depends on consistency.

Elsewhere, constraints should leave room for contextual adaptation.

### Design test

**Is this rule necessary for coherence, or are we encoding a local solution as a universal requirement?**

---

# 22. Hold the Domain, Not the Territory

The stewardship domain provides a shared field of attention and responsibility.

It should not imply ownership over all treasury data, analysis, evaluation, or learning.

Other groups should remain free to:

- maintain independent datasets;
- develop competing methods;
- publish alternative interpretations;
- create adjacent observatories;
- fork or extend shared infrastructure.

The Observatory's legitimacy should depend on usefulness and stewardship quality rather than exclusivity.

### Design test

**Does this mechanism strengthen a shared field of practice, or claim jurisdiction over activity that should remain open?**

---

# Working Synthesis

Several principles recur throughout this document:

> **Shared memory, plural sensemaking, temporary authority.**

> **Centralize what improves learning; distribute what requires judgment.**

> **Make uncertainty, provenance, and interpretation visible.**

> **Follow consequential uncertainty rather than precommitted deliverables.**

> **Let recurring need generate structure, and let experience revise it.**

> **Steward the commons' capacity to learn rather than attempting to become the commons' evaluator.**
