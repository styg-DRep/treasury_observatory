# Treasury Observatory — Concept Charter

## Status

**Formation-stage working charter**

This charter describes a possible stewardship domain in response to the Treasury Observatory Driver.

It does not establish a permanent institution, governance body, certification authority, or treasury mandate.

Its purpose is to make the proposed field of work explicit enough that it can be explored, challenged, and developed coherently.

---

# 1. Context

Public treasury activity produces information about proposals, costs, estimates, delivery, outcomes, and the behavior of the funding system.

Much of this information is distributed across separate processes and periods of activity. Participants therefore have limited shared infrastructure through which experience can accumulate into persistent, contestable learning about the funding commons.

The Treasury Observatory is a working concept for stewarding that learning capacity.

---

# 2. Stewardship Domain

## Working Definition

A **stewardship domain** is a field of conditions, resources, relationships, and practices for which participants intentionally coordinate care, learning, and responsibility without claiming exclusive ownership or centralized control.

A stewardship domain expresses:

- **accountability** without requiring singular ownership;
- **authority** that is bounded, explicit, and situated rather than totalizing;
- **participation** that may remain distributed across many actors;
- **empiricism** through observation of effects and revision in response to experience;
- **continuity** through shared records, methods, and agreements;
- **contestability** by keeping claims, methods, and interpretations open to challenge;
- **care for the commons** as distinct from control over the commons.

The term does not imply that there is one correct way to define stewardship or domain boundaries. Other participants may reasonably use different language or draw different boundaries.

Its purpose here is pragmatic: to name a topology of responsibility that allows people to coordinate around a shared field of concern without presuming that a centralized organization must own or govern that field.

## The Observatory's Stewardship Domain

The Treasury Observatory concerns itself with:

> **the shared capacity to observe, preserve, compare, interpret, and learn from evidence about the behavior of a decentralized public funding commons.**

Its domain includes the stewardship of:

- evidence structures;
- historical memory;
- methods of comparison and estimation;
- uncertainty representations;
- reference classes;
- analytical provenance;
- competing interpretations;
- developmental inquiries;
- learning generated through treasury activity;
- observations about the effects of the Observatory's own methods and practices.

The Observatory does not own this domain.

It holds a responsibility to make participation in the domain more coherent, transparent, and generative.

---

# 3. Intended Contribution

The Observatory seeks to make it easier for treasury participants to ask and investigate questions such as:

- What does comparable work appear to cost?
- What assumptions are carrying a cost estimate?
- How have previous estimates compared with later experience?
- Where are useful reference classes available, and where are they weak?
- What patterns are emerging across funding activity?
- How are funding processes influencing proposer and market behavior?
- Which effects appear local to individual projects and which may be systemic?
- What remains uncertain?
- What competing explanations fit the available evidence?
- What could be learned through further observation or experimentation?

Its contribution is not to resolve these questions on behalf of the ecosystem, but to improve the conditions under which they can be investigated.

---

# 4. Core Functions

The working concept currently includes four related functions.

## Evidence Stewardship

Support durable, traceable records of observations relevant to treasury activity.

This may include:

- proposal information;
- estimates;
- cost structures;
- funding history;
- milestones;
- changes in scope;
- expenditure information where available;
- delivery evidence;
- outcomes;
- external reference data;
- later observations relevant to previous claims.

Evidence should retain provenance wherever practicable.

## Methods Stewardship

Support explicit and inspectable ways of transforming evidence into analytical claims.

Possible methods include:

- cost decomposition;
- external benchmarking;
- reference-class forecasting;
- estimate-to-actual comparison;
- uncertainty analysis;
- calibration analysis;
- concentration analysis;
- market comparison;
- portfolio analysis.

Methods may coexist, compete, change, or be deprecated.

The Observatory should make method choice visible rather than implying that one method represents the domain itself.

## Inquiry Stewardship

Support bounded inquiry into tensions, anomalies, recurring patterns, or emerging questions.

An inquiry may bring together:

- observations;
- competing hypotheses;
- contextual knowledge;
- external evidence;
- interpretations from affected participants;
- possible experiments or adaptations.

Inquiries should remain distinguishable from decisions about treasury allocation.

## Reflexive Stewardship

Observe the effects produced by the Observatory itself.

This includes attention to questions such as:

- Do published benchmarks influence pricing?
- Do assurance practices increase participation costs?
- Do particular methods privilege certain forms of work?
- Are analytical outputs becoming substitutes for participant judgment?
- Are contributors accumulating informal authority that is not visible in governance structures?
- Are novel or poorly comparable proposals being represented appropriately?
- Is the Observatory creating useful learning relative to the burden it introduces?

The Observatory is therefore included within its own field of observation.

---

# 5. Relationship to Developmental Evaluation

The Observatory treats evaluation as an ongoing function distributed across the funding commons rather than as an activity performed exclusively by a designated evaluator.

Developmental evaluation is expressed through the repeated movement between:

**observation → interpretation → hypothesis → experiment or adaptation → further observation**

Different participants may contribute at different points in this cycle.

The Observatory supports this distributed function by providing shared memory, explicit methods, visible provenance, inquiry practices, and opportunities for competing interpretations to encounter common evidence.

It does not require consensus about what the evidence means.

---

# 6. Relationship to Governance

The Observatory is intended to support governance without becoming an allocation authority.

It may inform:

- proposers;
- DReps and delegates;
- voters;
- treasury administrators;
- auditors;
- maintainers;
- researchers;
- funding bodies;
- other participants in treasury stewardship.

It does not, by virtue of this charter:

- approve or reject proposals;
- establish mandatory prices;
- determine whether an initiative is valuable;
- establish binding treasury policy;
- replace domain expertise;
- certify individuals as authoritative evaluators.

Governance participants remain responsible for judgment and decision-making within the authorities they already hold.

---

# 7. Relationship to Cost

Cost is expected to be the Observatory's first major inquiry domain because treasury participants frequently need to reason about cost under conditions of uncertainty and incomplete comparability.

The Observatory may support questions about:

- estimate plausibility;
- cost decomposition;
- external comparables;
- historical reference classes;
- uncertainty ranges;
- estimate calibration;
- treasury-specific cost conditions;
- changes in market pricing.

Cost analysis does not determine public value.

A proposal can be well-estimated and undesirable, poorly estimated and potentially valuable, or appropriately uncertain because the work itself is novel.

Cost confidence and value judgment therefore remain distinct.

---

# 8. Participation and Authority

Participation in observation, analysis, interpretation, and inquiry should remain as open as practicable.

Credibility should arise primarily from:

- evidence quality;
- methodological transparency;
- provenance;
- reproducibility;
- demonstrated calibration where relevant;
- constructive engagement with competing explanations.

Authority should remain bounded and contextual.

Where temporary authority is useful—for example, to steward an inquiry—it should be explicit in scope and duration.

A useful working principle is:

> **shared memory, plural sensemaking, temporary authority**

---

# 9. Stewardship Principles

The emerging Observatory should seek to preserve:

### Observation before judgment
Describe conditions before deciding what they mean.

### Explicit uncertainty
Represent uncertainty rather than converting it prematurely into apparent precision.

### Provenance
Make it possible to understand where claims and transformations came from.

### Method transparency
Analytical outputs should expose the method by which they were produced.

### Plural interpretation
Shared evidence does not require singular interpretation.

### Contestability
Claims should remain open to challenge, reproduction, refinement, or replacement.

### Separation of evidence and allocation authority
Providing information about a proposal should not itself confer power to decide its funding.

### Developmental orientation
Methods and structures remain open to adaptation as the funding commons changes.

### Reflexivity
The Observatory's own effects are legitimate objects of inquiry.

### Proportionality
The burden of observation and assurance should be considered alongside the value of the information produced.

---

# 10. Initial Boundaries

During formation, the Observatory is not assumed to be:

- a treasury office;
- an auditing firm;
- a rating agency;
- a proposal-review committee;
- a grants council;
- a price-setting institution;
- a centralized data authority;
- a permanent evaluator corps.

Any of these functions may overlap with individual activities, but none defines the concept.

---

# 11. Formation Questions

Several questions remain intentionally unresolved.

### Institutional form
Is the Observatory best understood as a protocol, commons, network, registry, research function, or hybrid?

### Scope
Should cost remain the primary domain, or should delivery, outcomes, market effects, and governance effects be represented from the beginning?

### Data
Which historical treasury records are sufficiently available and comparable to support useful inquiry?

### Methods
Which analytical methods produce useful information without creating false precision?

### Participation
How can contributors earn contextual credibility without creating an expert caste?

### Governance
What is the minimum stewardship structure required to maintain continuity without accumulating unnecessary authority?

### Sustainability
What level of ongoing funding and maintenance would be justified by the learning generated?

### Legitimacy
How should the Observatory relate to existing treasury processes without requiring monopoly status or formal adoption?

---

# 12. Formation Intent

The immediate purpose is not to establish the Observatory.

It is to determine whether this stewardship domain can generate enough useful learning to warrant a more durable institutional form.

Formation work should therefore favor:

- inquiry over premature specification;
- prototypes over comprehensive architecture;
- real treasury evidence over hypothetical models;
- reversible experiments over durable bureaucracy;
- explicit learning records over claims of completion.

The Observatory should become more defined only where experience provides reason to define it.
