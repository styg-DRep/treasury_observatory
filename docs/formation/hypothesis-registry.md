# Treasury Observatory — Hypothesis Registry

## Status

**Formation-stage learning infrastructure**

This Registry tracks consequential hypotheses whose validity materially affects the design, legitimacy, continuation, or evolution of the Treasury Observatory.

It is not a record of every hypothesis produced by every inquiry or experiment.

Local hypotheses should normally remain attached to the activity that tests them. The Registry holds the higher-order hypotheses that define the Observatory's current institutional bets.

---

# 1. Purpose

The Registry exists to make consequential assumptions:

- explicit;
- inspectable;
- testable;
- revisable;
- historically traceable.

Its purpose is not to convert uncertainty into premature certainty.

It provides a shared record of:

> what the Observatory currently expects to be true,  
> why those expectations matter,  
> what evidence bears on them,  
> how confidence changes over time,  
> and what conditions should cause reconsideration.

The Registry is therefore part of the Observatory's developmental evaluation function.

---

# 2. What Belongs in the Registry

A hypothesis belongs in the Registry when its validity materially affects one or more of:

- the Observatory's stewardship domain;
- its institutional form;
- its legitimacy;
- its major design principles;
- its methods;
- its sustainability;
- the burden it places on the commons;
- the continuation or retirement of a significant Observatory function.

Examples include:

> Shared historical evidence can improve treasury sensemaking.

> Temporary inquiry structures can provide useful evaluation without creating a permanent evaluator corps.

> Public calibration can support contextual credibility without requiring centralized accreditation.

These are **formation or institutional hypotheses**.

---

# 3. What Does Not Normally Belong in the Registry

The Registry should not absorb every local proposition.

An experiment might test:

> Schema v0.2 will allow three analysts to independently classify at least 80% of sampled proposal costs into the same categories.

That hypothesis belongs with the experiment.

An inquiry might consider:

> Rising infrastructure costs are primarily driven by specialist labor scarcity.

That hypothesis belongs with the inquiry.

Such local hypotheses may reference a parent Registry hypothesis and may generate evidence that updates it.

---

# 4. Relationship Between Registered and Local Hypotheses

The working model is:

**Registered hypothesis**  
A consequential assumption about the Observatory or its stewardship approach.

**Local hypothesis**  
A bounded claim tested within an experiment, inquiry, prototype, funding request, or other activity.

**Observation**  
Evidence generated by activity.

**Learning update**  
A change in confidence, framing, scope, or validity context resulting from accumulated evidence.

Local hypotheses should travel with the activity that tests them.

Registered hypotheses persist across activities.

---

# 5. Hypothesis Record

Each Registry entry should contain:

## Identifier

A stable identifier such as:

**H01**

Identifiers should not be reused.

---

## Hypothesis

A concise, falsifiable or meaningfully challengeable proposition.

The wording should avoid embedding conclusions that the available evidence cannot yet support.

---

## Why It Matters

What would change if the hypothesis were substantially supported, weakened, or invalidated?

This section establishes why the claim deserves institutional attention.

---

## Current Confidence

A lightweight qualitative state:

- **Unexamined**
- **Tentative**
- **Emerging support**
- **Mixed**
- **Strong support**
- **Substantially weakened**

These states describe the current evidence position.

They are not probabilities and should not imply mathematical precision where none exists.

---

## Evidence For

Links or references to observations, inquiries, experiments, or other evidence that currently support the hypothesis.

---

## Evidence Against / Complicating Evidence

Evidence that weakens the hypothesis, limits its scope, reveals side effects, or suggests competing explanations.

---

## Competing or Adjacent Hypotheses

Alternative explanations or formulations that should remain visible.

A registered hypothesis should not become more credible merely because alternatives were not recorded.

---

## Validity Context

The conditions under which the hypothesis is currently understood to apply.

This may include:

- treasury architecture;
- governance process;
- funding scale;
- voluntary or mandatory use;
- technological conditions;
- market context;
- maturity of Observatory infrastructure.

A hypothesis may be well supported in one context without being generalizable to another.

---

## Review Triggers

Conditions that should cause the hypothesis to be revisited.

These may include:

- evidence triggers;
- experiment triggers;
- context triggers;
- temporal or activity triggers.

---

## Last Learning Update

A dated summary of the most recent meaningful change in understanding.

---

## History

A short append-only record of major state changes, reframings, mergers, splits, or retirement.

---

# 6. Hypothesis Lifecycle

A hypothesis may move through the following lifecycle:

**Proposed**  
The hypothesis has been entered but not yet accepted as important enough for active tracking.

**Active**  
The hypothesis materially informs formation work and should accumulate evidence.

**Supported**  
Evidence currently gives the Observatory substantial reason to continue acting as if the hypothesis is useful.

**Mixed**  
Evidence supports some parts or contexts while weakening others.

**Reframed**  
The original formulation is no longer adequate and has been replaced by a more precise claim.

**Substantially weakened**  
Available evidence no longer supports using the hypothesis as a significant design assumption.

**Retired**  
The hypothesis is no longer relevant, has been superseded, or no longer warrants active attention.

Retirement should preserve the historical record.

The Registry should not erase hypotheses simply because they proved unhelpful.

---

# 7. Update Triggers

The Registry should be updated because relevant conditions have changed, not merely because a calendar date arrived.

Four trigger families are recognized.

## Evidence Trigger

New evidence materially supports, weakens, complicates, or narrows a hypothesis.

Examples:

- estimate-to-actual data becomes available;
- repeated use reveals an unintended effect;
- an external comparison materially changes interpretation.

---

## Experiment Trigger

A deliberately designed probe concludes and produces evidence relevant to the hypothesis.

The Registry should record the learning generated, not merely whether the experiment "succeeded."

---

## Context Trigger

A material condition underlying the hypothesis changes.

Examples:

- treasury governance architecture changes;
- an Observatory method becomes mandatory;
- a major market shift invalidates prior reference classes;
- a funding process is replaced.

---

## Temporal or Activity Trigger

Enough time or activity has accumulated that the hypothesis should be reconsidered even without an obvious event.

Examples:

- after 20 completed assessments;
- after 10 inquiry cells;
- after two treasury cycles;
- after 12 months of public benchmark use.

These triggers are particularly useful for detecting predicted effects that have failed to appear.

---

# 8. Updating a Hypothesis

An update should answer five questions:

1. **What changed?**
2. **What evidence produced the change?**
3. **Does the hypothesis itself need reframing?**
4. **Did the validity context change?**
5. **What should happen next?**

Updates should not require consensus that the hypothesis is "true."

Where interpretations differ, the Registry may preserve multiple readings of the evidence.

---

# 9. Reframing, Splitting, and Merging

Some hypotheses will prove too broad.

For example:

> Historical evidence improves treasury judgment.

may later divide into:

> Historical internal reference classes improve estimation calibration.

and:

> Historical internal reference classes can create anchoring effects.

When this occurs, the original hypothesis should be marked **Reframed** and linked to its successors.

Similarly, overlapping hypotheses may be merged where doing so improves clarity without erasing useful distinctions.

The historical lineage should remain visible.

---

# 10. Relationship to Experiments and Inquiries

Every significant experiment should state:

- its purpose;
- any parent Registry hypothesis;
- its local hypothesis or hypotheses;
- the probe being conducted;
- signals that would support, weaken, or complicate the local hypothesis;
- resulting learning;
- implications for the parent hypothesis.

Every significant inquiry should state:

- the tension or question being explored;
- any parent Registry hypotheses;
- competing local hypotheses;
- evidence considered;
- what changed in understanding;
- what remains unresolved.

The Registry should link to these activities rather than reproduce their full contents.

---

# 11. Relationship to Funding and Sustainability Requests

A funding or sustainability request may itself function as an experiment.

Such a request should carry its own intervention hypothesis, for example:

> Continued operation for another treasury cycle will generate enough decision-useful learning to justify the additional stewardship cost.

Where this bears on a registered institutional hypothesis, the relationship should be explicit.

Funding should therefore generate learning about the Observatory's own sustainability model, not merely finance continuation.

---

# 12. Registry Stewardship

The Registry requires continuity but should not require a centralized evaluator.

Stewardship responsibilities include:

- maintaining identifiers and history;
- recording evidence links;
- initiating review when explicit triggers occur;
- ensuring competing evidence is not silently omitted;
- facilitating reframing when hypotheses become inadequate;
- preserving retired hypotheses.

Registry stewardship does not confer authority to determine whether hypotheses are correct.

Its responsibility is to keep institutional learning visible and coherent.

# 13. Initial Formation Hypotheses

## H01 — Durable Shared Memory

### Hypothesis

A durable shared record of treasury evidence, methods, interpretations, and subsequent outcomes can improve the commons' ability to carry learning across funding decisions and governance cycles.

### Why It Matters

This is a foundational justification for the Observatory.

If persistent shared memory does not materially improve future sensemaking, a major portion of the stewardship domain may be unnecessary.

### Current Confidence

**Tentative — strengthened by HTEP-001 evidence handoffs; downstream reuse not tested**

### Initial Evidence

The current formation work has identified repeated fragmentation between proposals, historical funding records, delivery evidence, and later governance judgment.

### Complicating Evidence

Accumulating information does not necessarily produce learning. Large repositories may increase burden or noise without improving judgment.

### Competing Hypothesis

The primary constraint may be attention and interpretation rather than access to historical evidence.

### Validity Context

Decentralized treasury activity where relevant records exist but are distributed across multiple systems and governance periods.

### Review Trigger

After the first historical evidence prototype and at least one real governance use case.

---

### Last Learning Update

**2026-11-06 — HTEP-001 qualified closure:** Six independently reviewed Corpus handoffs with source provenance, stable identifiers and documented source gaps support the feasibility of shared historical memory. No evidence yet that the records improve participant decisions in practice.

### History

- HTEP-001 closure, 2026-11-06: tentative hypothesis retained with stronger feasibility evidence; a future independent-use test is still needed.

## H02 — Longitudinal Integration Improves Sensemaking

### Hypothesis

Connecting evidence across time can help treasury participants reason about trajectories, rates of change, persistence, and prior interventions more effectively than relying primarily on present-state snapshots.

### Why It Matters

A major proposed value of the Observatory is its ability to turn repeated treasury observations into longitudinal understanding.

### Current Confidence

**Tentative — strengthened by exploratory HTEP-001 trajectories**

### Initial Evidence

Historical funding and cost data can in principle reveal patterns that are not visible in isolated proposals.

### Complicating Evidence

Longitudinal data can conceal structural breaks if changes in context are not represented.

A long time series may create an appearance of continuity where the underlying system has changed.

### Competing Hypothesis

Recent context may sometimes be more decision-relevant than historical trajectory.

### Validity Context

Questions involving recurring funding activity, repeated project classes, evolving prices, or repeated governance interventions.

### Review Trigger

After at least one inquiry compares snapshot-based and trajectory-based interpretation of the same treasury condition.

---

### Last Learning Update

**2026-11-06 — HTEP-001 qualified closure:** Amaru continuation, Eternl revision, Liqwid follow-up and Orion delegation reveal otherwise obscured funding relationships. Their decision relevance and generalizability have not been tested with independent participants.

### History

- HTEP-001 closure, 2026-11-06: exploratory support added; retain caution about misleading continuity across structural breaks.

## H03 — Shared Evidence Can Support Plural Interpretation

### Hypothesis

A common evidence base can improve distributed sensemaking without requiring convergence on a single official interpretation.

### Why It Matters

The Observatory depends on combining shared memory with decentralized judgment.

If common evidence repeatedly produces pressure for singular interpretation, additional institutional safeguards may be required.

### Current Confidence

**Tentative**

### Complicating Evidence

Dashboards, rankings, and simplified summaries may cause one interpretation to dominate even where underlying evidence remains open.

### Competing Hypothesis

Effective governance may require stronger synthesis than plural interpretation alone can provide.

### Review Trigger

After several analyses of the same evidence base are produced by independent participants.

---

## H04 — Method Transparency Can Substitute for Some Institutional Trust

### Hypothesis

Making analytical methods, assumptions, transformations, and limitations inspectable can allow participants to evaluate claims with less dependence on institutional authority or evaluator status.

### Why It Matters

This supports the Observatory's preference for transparent methods over centralized credentialing.

### Current Confidence

**Tentative**

### Complicating Evidence

Many participants may not have the time or expertise to inspect complex methods and may continue relying on trusted intermediaries.

### Competing Hypothesis

Transparent methods and trusted interpreters may be complements rather than substitutes.

### Review Trigger

After prototype assessments have been used by participants with differing levels of technical expertise.

---

## H05 — Public Calibration Can Support Contextual Credibility

### Hypothesis

Where outcomes can later be observed, publishing the calibration and historical performance of estimates or analytical claims can produce useful credibility signals without requiring centralized certification of evaluators.

### Why It Matters

This is a possible mechanism for distributed accountability and contextual expertise.

### Current Confidence

**Tentative**

### Complicating Evidence

Calibration may be difficult where outcomes are ambiguous, delayed, or affected by changing scope.

Metrics may also be gamed if they become status signals.

### Competing Hypothesis

Peer review, demonstrated domain contribution, or other credibility mechanisms may be more useful than quantitative calibration in some domains.

### Review Trigger

After enough assessments have corresponding realized outcomes to make calibration meaningful.

---

## H06 — Historical Reference Classes Can Improve Cost Reasoning Without Becoming Price Rules

### Hypothesis

Historical treasury and external market data can support useful cost reference classes while remaining evidence for judgment rather than normative price-setting.

### Why It Matters

This is central to the initial Cost Observatory concept.

### Current Confidence

**Tentative — materially constrained by HTEP-001 cost-evidence limitations**

### Complicating Evidence

Published benchmarks may anchor future proposals or legitimize historically distorted prices.

### Competing Hypothesis

External market comparables or bottom-up estimates may need to remain primary, with treasury history serving only a secondary role.

### Review Trigger

After prototype reference classes are used in live or simulated proposal assessments.

## H07 — Explicit Uncertainty Improves Decision Quality

### Hypothesis

Representing ranges, assumptions, sensitivity, comparability, and known uncertainty can improve treasury judgment relative to presenting single-point estimates alone.

### Why It Matters

The Observatory is designed for domains where uncertainty is often inherent rather than merely a defect.

### Current Confidence

**Tentative**

### Complicating Evidence

More nuanced uncertainty representations may overwhelm participants or be reduced informally to a single score.

### Competing Hypothesis

Some governance contexts may require intentionally simplified representations to remain usable.

### Review Trigger

After different uncertainty formats have been tested with actual governance participants.

---

## H08 — Temporary Inquiry Structures Can Support Distributed Developmental Evaluation

### Hypothesis

Bounded, temporary inquiry structures can investigate emerging treasury tensions and generate useful learning without requiring a permanent evaluator role or standing evaluation bureaucracy.

### Why It Matters

This is a central institutional design claim.

### Current Confidence

**Tentative**

### Complicating Evidence

Some inquiries may require durable domain expertise or continuity that temporary structures cannot provide efficiently.

### Competing Hypothesis

A small permanent analytical function may be necessary alongside temporary inquiry structures.

### Review Trigger

After multiple completed inquiry cycles of differing complexity.

---

## H09 — Bounded Authority Can Preserve Coordination Without Centralizing Judgment

### Hypothesis

Explicitly scoped, reviewable, and time-bounded authority can provide enough coordination for stewardship activities while preserving decentralized judgment over treasury decisions.

### Why It Matters

The Observatory requires continuity and coordination but seeks to avoid becoming an epistemic gatekeeper.

### Current Confidence

**Tentative**

### Complicating Evidence

Informal authority may accumulate around recurring contributors regardless of formal limits.

### Competing Hypothesis

Some functions may require durable delegated authority to remain reliable.

### Review Trigger

When recurring roles emerge or when the same contributors repeatedly hold temporary authority.

---

## H10 — Cost Assurance Can Remain Distinct from Value Judgment

### Hypothesis

The Observatory can provide useful cost-confidence information without that information becoming a proxy for whether a proposal should be funded.

### Why It Matters

Cost is likely to be the first major observational domain, but the Observatory should not transform cost legibility into funding authority.

### Current Confidence

**Tentative**

### Complicating Evidence

Governance participants may use confidence scores heuristically as approval or rejection signals.

### Competing Hypothesis

Any standardized cost-assurance output will inevitably acquire some decision-weight beyond its intended scope.

### Review Trigger

After cost-assurance artifacts have been used in real or simulated treasury deliberation.

---

## H11 — Observatory Burden Can Remain Proportionate

### Hypothesis

The learning and assurance generated by the Observatory can be produced without imposing participation, reporting, or analytical costs that materially outweigh their value.

### Why It Matters

An Observatory that substantially increases proposal or governance burden could reproduce the inefficiencies it seeks to illuminate.

### Current Confidence

**Tentative — qualitative stewardship evidence; quantitative burden untested**

### Complicating Evidence

High-quality longitudinal data often requires sustained reporting discipline.

### Competing Hypothesis

Some valuable forms of evidence may only be available through relatively costly reporting requirements.

### Review Trigger

Every major prototype or process should record the burden it creates.

## H12 — Observatory Outputs Can Increase Commons Capacity Rather Than Dependency

### Hypothesis

The Observatory can leave participants with greater independent capacity to reason about treasury activity rather than increasing dependence on a specialized institution or expert class.

### Why It Matters

This is one of the strongest tests of whether the Observatory behaves as a commons institution.

### Current Confidence

**Tentative — early institutional signal, cold-start reuse untested**

### Complicating Evidence

Specialized analytical infrastructure naturally creates knowledge asymmetries.

### Competing Hypothesis

Some institutional dependency may be unavoidable where the analytical domain becomes sufficiently technical.

### Review Trigger

After recurring Observatory use, assess whether methods and reasoning are diffusing beyond core contributors.

## H13 — Reflexive Evaluation Can Detect Observatory-Induced Distortion

### Hypothesis

Explicitly observing the Observatory's own effects can reveal meaningful distortions such as benchmark anchoring, participation barriers, metric gaming, or accumulation of informal authority early enough to support adaptation.

### Why It Matters

The Observatory is not outside the system it observes.

### Current Confidence

**Unexamined**

### Complicating Evidence

Institutions are often poor at recognizing effects that sustain their own legitimacy or funding.

### Competing Hypothesis

Meaningful reflexivity may require independent external critique in addition to internal observation.

### Review Trigger

At the conclusion of major experiments, after material method adoption, and during sustainability review.

---

## H14 — Voluntary Use Can Generate Sufficient Legitimacy and Utility

### Hypothesis

The Observatory can become useful through voluntary adoption and demonstrated value without requiring monopoly status, mandatory treasury integration, or exclusive authority.

### Why It Matters

This affects the institutional form and relationship to existing governance structures.

### Current Confidence

**Unexamined**

### Complicating Evidence

Voluntary systems may be ignored precisely where better evidence is most needed.

### Competing Hypothesis

Some minimum integration with treasury processes may eventually be necessary to generate reliable data and impact.

### Review Trigger

After formation-stage outputs have been offered to treasury participants without mandatory adoption.

# 14. Initial Registry-Level Learning Questions

The first formation phase should pay particular attention to several cross-cutting questions:

- Which hypotheses appear strongly coupled and should perhaps be reframed together?
- Which hypotheses are actually testable during formation?
- Which require longer operational history?
- Which depend on data that may not exist?
- Which design principles are resting on the weakest assumptions?
- Which hypotheses, if weakened, would materially change the case for an Observatory?
- Which risks would still matter even if all primary hypotheses were supported?

These questions should help determine the first experiment and inquiry backlog.

---

# 15. Working Principle

The Registry should not become a collection of beliefs the institution seeks to defend.

Its purpose is the opposite:

> **make consequential assumptions visible enough that experience can change them.**
