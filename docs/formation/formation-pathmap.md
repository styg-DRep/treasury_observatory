# Treasury Observatory — Formation Pathmap

## Status

**Adaptive formation guidance**

The Formation Pathmap identifies the most consequential uncertainties currently facing the Treasury Observatory concept and maps plausible routes for learning about them.

It is not a roadmap.

It does not prescribe a fixed sequence of deliverables, dates, or institutional milestones.

Its purpose is to help the Formation Inquiry choose the next useful work based on what is currently unknown and what previous work reveals.

---

# 1. Relationship to the Formation Inquiry

The **Formation Inquiry** is the overarching developmental inquiry:

> **Can a Treasury Observatory materially improve stewardship of a decentralized public funding commons, and what is the smallest institutional form capable of doing so without displacing plural judgment or creating unnecessary authority?**

The Formation Pathmap supports that inquiry.

It maps:

- consequential institutional hypotheses;
- dependencies among them;
- what can be tested now;
- what requires longer observation;
- candidate inquiries and experiments;
- branch conditions that could change the direction of formation work.

Individual inquiries and experiments sit beneath the Pathmap and generate evidence back into it.

---

# 2. Pathmapping Principle

The Formation Pathmap follows one primary effectiveness principle:

> **Prefer the available next action that most reduces consequential uncertainty about the viability or shape of the Observatory.**

A useful next step therefore tends to combine:

### Consequence
If the underlying hypothesis is substantially wrong, would the Observatory need to change materially?

### Early Testability
Can meaningful evidence be generated under current formation conditions?

### Dependency
Do other important hypotheses rely on this one?

### Learning Leverage
Could investigating this question inform several parts of the design at once?

These dimensions guide attention.

They are not intended to produce a mechanically authoritative score.

---

# 3. Formation Horizon

The Pathmap currently sees four broad learning territories.

They are not fixed phases.

Movement between them may be recursive, parallel, or abandoned depending on what is learned.

## Territory A — Can Shared Evidence Produce Useful Learning?

Primary hypotheses:

- H01 — Durable Shared Memory
- H02 — Longitudinal Integration Improves Sensemaking
- H06 — Historical Reference Classes Can Improve Cost Reasoning

Central uncertainty:

> **Can existing and prospectively structured treasury evidence become meaningfully more useful when integrated across proposals, funding cycles, and time?**

This is foundational.

If useful shared evidence cannot be constructed, much of the Observatory concept changes.

---

## Territory B — Can Analysis Improve Judgment Without Becoming Authority?

Primary hypotheses:

- H03 — Shared Evidence Can Support Plural Interpretation
- H04 — Method Transparency Can Substitute for Some Institutional Trust
- H07 — Explicit Uncertainty Improves Decision Quality
- H10 — Cost Assurance Can Remain Distinct from Value Judgment

Central uncertainty:

> **Can structured analytical outputs increase the quality of treasury sensemaking without becoming de facto approval signals or epistemic gatekeeping mechanisms?**

This territory tests the Observatory's intended relationship to governance.

---

## Territory C — Can Evaluation Remain Distributed?

Primary hypotheses:

- H05 — Public Calibration Can Support Contextual Credibility
- H08 — Temporary Inquiry Structures Can Support Distributed Developmental Evaluation
- H09 — Bounded Authority Can Preserve Coordination Without Centralizing Judgment

Central uncertainty:

> **Can the evaluative function remain distributed while still providing enough continuity, coordination, expertise, and accountability to be useful?**

This territory begins testing institutional form.

---

## Territory D — Can the Observatory Remain a Healthy Commons Institution?

Primary hypotheses:

- H11 — Observatory Burden Can Remain Proportionate
- H12 — Observatory Outputs Can Increase Commons Capacity Rather Than Dependency
- H13 — Reflexive Evaluation Can Detect Observatory-Induced Distortion
- H14 — Voluntary Use Can Generate Sufficient Legitimacy and Utility

Central uncertainty:

> **Can the Observatory remain useful over time without producing excessive burden, institutional dependency, epistemic enclosure, or a need for monopoly authority?**

These questions are highly consequential but require accumulated operational experience.

# 6. First High-Leverage Probe

## Historical Treasury Evidence Probe

### Purpose

Determine whether historical treasury records can be transformed into sufficiently coherent shared evidence to support longitudinal sensemaking and cost inquiry.

### Parent Hypotheses

- H01 — Durable Shared Memory
- H02 — Longitudinal Integration
- H06 — Historical Reference Classes
- H11 — Proportionate Burden

### Initial Questions

Can a sample of prior treasury activity be represented consistently enough to compare:

- requested funding;
- time period;
- project type;
- major cost categories;
- assumptions;
- prior related funding;
- milestones;
- delivery information;
- later funding requests;
- known outcomes?

Does connecting these observations across time reveal information that is difficult to see proposal-by-proposal?

Which fields are reliably available?

Which require interpretation?

Which are too costly to reconstruct?

Where do apparent trajectories reflect genuine continuity, and where do changes in context create structural breaks?

### Candidate Probe

Select a bounded sample of historical treasury-funded activity and attempt to produce:

1. a minimal normalized evidence record;
2. one or more longitudinal views;
3. provisional cost reference classes where the evidence supports them;
4. an explicit record of missing or incomparable information.

### Learning Signals

The probe would be encouraging if it reveals:

- previously difficult-to-see trajectories;
- meaningful estimate or cost comparisons;
- recurring patterns;
- useful distinctions between project classes;
- information that participants consider decision-relevant.

It would complicate the concept if:

- records cannot be normalized without extensive subjective interpretation;
- historical context makes comparison misleading;
- reconstruction costs exceed likely informational value;
- useful trajectories remain rare even after significant work.

### Important Negative Result

A finding that historical records are insufficient would still be valuable.

It may imply that the Observatory should emphasize prospective evidence standards rather than retrospective reconstruction.

# 7. Likely Second Probe

If the Historical Evidence Probe produces decision-useful material, the next high-leverage question becomes:

> **How should that evidence be presented so that it supports judgment without creating false authority?**

A candidate **Assessment Prototype** could test several representations of the same proposal or historical case:

- raw evidence only;
- normalized cost breakdown;
- reference-class comparison;
- uncertainty range;
- explicit competing interpretations;
- composite confidence indicator.

This would bear on:

- H03;
- H04;
- H07;
- H10;
- H11.

The objective would not be to choose a polished product format.

It would be to discover how representation affects interpretation.

---

# 8. Likely Third Probe

If structured assessment proves useful, a temporary **Inquiry Cell** could investigate a real treasury tension using shared evidence.

Examples might include:

- rising infrastructure maintenance costs;
- repeated funding among incumbent recipients;
- estimate-to-actual variance;
- governance overhead associated with small grants;
- recurring emergency funding requests.

This would begin testing:

- H05;
- H08;
- H09;
- H12;
- H13.

The Inquiry Cell should itself be treated as an experiment in distributed developmental evaluation.

# 9. Branch Conditions

The Pathmap should explicitly recognize conditions that would redirect formation work.

## Branch A — Historical Evidence Is Highly Useful

If historical evidence supports meaningful longitudinal analysis:

→ develop stronger evidence objects and reference-class methods.

---

## Branch B — Historical Evidence Is Sparse but Recoverable

If historical evidence is useful but reconstruction is expensive:

→ explore minimum prospective reporting structures and automated data capture.

---

## Branch C — Historical Evidence Is Poorly Comparable

If historical cost comparison is unreliable:

→ shift emphasis toward bottom-up estimation, external comparables, and prospective calibration.

---

## Branch D — Analytical Outputs Create Strong Anchoring

If assessments repeatedly become de facto funding recommendations:

→ reduce composite scoring, increase interpretive plurality, or reconsider whether cost assurance should be standardized.

---

## Branch E — Temporary Inquiries Lack Continuity

If inquiry cells repeatedly recreate expertise or fail to retain learning:

→ investigate a small durable stewardship function while preserving bounded authority.

---

## Branch F — Observatory Burden Becomes Material

If data or assurance requirements create substantial participation costs:

→ simplify, tier, automate, or discontinue low-value requirements.

---

## Branch G — Commons Capacity Increases

If methods and reasoning diffuse beyond Observatory contributors:

→ emphasize open tooling, training, reproducibility, and decentralized reuse.

---

## Branch H — Institutional Dependency Increases

If governance increasingly defers to Observatory outputs or personnel:

→ investigate mechanisms for decentralizing analysis, multiplying providers, or reducing institutional authority.

---

# 10. Continuous Cross-Cutting Observations

Several hypotheses should be observed throughout formation rather than deferred to a later phase.

## H11 — Proportionate Burden

Every experiment should record:

- time required;
- specialist skill required;
- data preparation burden;
- participant burden;
- maintenance implications.

---

## H13 — Reflexivity

Every experiment should ask:

> What behavior might this intervention itself be changing?

---

## H12 — Capacity vs Dependency

Formation work should look for early signals of whether methods are becoming reusable by others or increasingly specialized within a small contributor group.

---

# 11. Pathmap Update Triggers

The Pathmap should be revised when:

- a Registry hypothesis is materially reframed;
- an experiment produces a significant negative or unexpected result;
- a new dependency among hypotheses becomes visible;
- a branch condition is reached;
- a previously untestable high-consequence hypothesis becomes testable;
- treasury conditions materially change;
- formation work reveals that a current path is producing little additional learning.

Changes to the Pathmap should record the reason for redirection.

A change of path is not evidence of failure.

It is evidence that the Formation Inquiry is functioning developmentally.

---

# 12. Relationship to Roadmapping

A roadmap typically asks:

> **What will we deliver, and when?**

The Formation Pathmap asks:

> **What do we most need to learn next, what could we do to learn it, and how might different findings change our direction?**

Roadmaps may eventually become useful for bounded implementation work.

The Pathmap remains appropriate while the institutional form itself is still under inquiry.
