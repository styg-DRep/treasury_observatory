# AP-001 — Prospective Evidence & Assessment Prototype

**Status:** Proposed  
**Formation issue:** #7 — Can an Assessment Prototype improve judgment without becoming authority?  
**Parent inquiry:** Treasury Observatory Formation Inquiry  
**Predecessor:** HTEP-001 — Historical Treasury Evidence Probe (Closed)

## 1. Probe Driver

The Treasury Observatory seeks to strengthen the funding commons' durable, shared, contestable capacity to learn from treasury activity while preserving plural judgment and decentralized decision-making.

HTEP-001 demonstrated that historical treasury evidence can support longitudinal understanding when provenance, economic distinctions, uncertainty, and relationships are preserved. It also exposed significant limits to retrospective reconstruction: missing source material, incomplete cost information, uncertain financial meanings, and substantial interpretive effort.

These limitations suggest that the Observatory's long-term contribution may depend as much on how evidence is generated **during proposal creation and decision-making** as on how historical evidence is recovered.

The Formation Inquiry therefore needs to learn whether a small, transparent evidence-and-interpretation practice can improve participants' understanding of a current proposal while producing more reusable evidence for future learning, without creating de facto assessment authority or disproportionate participation burden.

**The purpose is not merely to present historical evidence more effectively. It is to investigate whether learning from history can improve the conditions under which present treasury activity becomes future shared evidence.**

## 2. Probe Domain

This probe concerns:

> **The experimental capture, representation, and plural interpretation of treasury proposal evidence across a bounded transition from historical learning to prospective proposal formation and deliberation.**

The probe may:

- use existing Corpus evidence to identify relevant distinctions and test representations;
- experiment with lightweight evidence intake for one current or forming proposal;
- compare alternative representations of the same evidence;
- observe how participants distinguish evidence, assumptions, interpretations, and judgments;
- investigate the relationship between immediate decision usefulness and future evidence reusability;
- record participant burden, interpretive divergence, and possible authority effects;
- generate reusable evidence and learning records.

The probe does not authorize:

- an ongoing proposal intake service;
- a production assessment application;
- mandatory proposer reporting requirements;
- proposal rankings or quality scores;
- funding recommendations;
- binding price or cost standards;
- reference-class claims unsupported by evidence;
- composite confidence indicators;
- an official Observatory position on any proposal;
- representation of participating DReps' views as institutional judgments.

All activities remain voluntary, bounded, and reversible.

## 3. Relationship to Issue #7

Issue #7 proposes investigating whether different transparent, uncertainty-aware representations of independently preserved treasury evidence improve participant understanding without introducing false comparability, anchoring, or assessment authority.

This agreement preserves that inquiry.

It proposes a deliberate methodological refinement.

Rather than limiting the experiment to two historical histories, AP-001 uses a two-part design:

1. **Retrospective calibration:** Use one existing Corpus history to test a small set of representations and identify obvious interpretation or authority hazards.
2. **Prospective shadow intake:** Apply the resulting distinctions to one current or forming treasury proposal, testing usefulness to actual participants and the quality of evidence preserved for future learning.

The historical case is a calibration resource, not a presumed normative reference class for the contemporary proposal.

The prospective exercise is conducted in *shadow mode*: it does not constitute an authorized treasury assessment or intervention in governance.

The second part is conditional on a learning gate. It is not automatically activated merely because the first part produces an artifact.

This departure from Issue #7's illustrative two-history boundary is proposed for review, not presented as an already accepted amendment.

### Why this refinement matters

The Observatory Driver is concerned with the commons' ability to carry learning **across funding decisions and governance cycles**.

A historical-only representation exercise can test presentation effects but cannot directly test whether evidence captured during proposal creation becomes more useful to present participants or more reusable by future participants.

AP-001 therefore seeks to test both dimensions within one small experimental agreement.

## 4. Parent Formation Hypotheses

### Primary

**H03 — Shared Evidence Can Support Plural Interpretation**

Participants can reason from common evidence without needing to adopt one official interpretation.

**H04 — Method Transparency Can Substitute for Some Institutional Trust**

Visible sources, transformations, assumptions, and limitations can reduce reliance on the authority of an evaluator.

**H07 — Explicit Uncertainty Improves Decision Quality**

Uncertainty-aware representations may improve understanding relative to evidence presented without its relevant qualifications.

**H10 — Cost Assurance Can Remain Distinct from Value Judgment**

Cost-related evidence can inform judgment without being converted into a proposal approval signal.

### Additional and cross-cutting

**H01 — Durable Shared Memory:** Prospective evidence capture may improve the information available for future inquiry.

**H02 — Longitudinal Integration:** Connecting proposal evidence to later observations may support learning across time.

**H11 — Proportionate Burden:** Additional structure must generate usefulness proportionate to the effort it requires.

**H12 — Commons Capacity Rather Than Dependency:** The method should enable independent participants to interpret evidence without requiring Observatory analysts.

**H13 — Reflexive Evaluation:** The experiment should observe whether its own representations induce anchoring, deference, framing effects, or other distortions.

AP-001 does not claim to resolve these hypotheses. Its results may support, complicate, narrow, or redirect them.

## 5. Local Learning Hypotheses

### AP-P1 — Representation and comprehension

Lightly structured evidence with visible provenance and uncertainty can improve participants' ability to identify what is known, inferred, disputed, or missing relative to unassisted source reading.

### AP-P2 — Plural interpretation

A shared evidence representation can support distinguishable, independently articulated interpretations without requiring a single Observatory conclusion.

### AP-P3 — Prospective usefulness

Some distinctions discovered through historical reconstruction will be useful to participants reasoning about a current proposal during its creation or deliberation.

### AP-P4 — Future evidence quality

A bounded prospective intake can preserve useful proposal assumptions, economic meanings, uncertainty, and provenance that would otherwise be difficult to reconstruct later.

This hypothesis concerns prospective evidence quality, not realized improvement in future historical analysis, which cannot yet be observed.

### AP-P5 — Proportionate effort

The additional evidence and interpretive usefulness can be generated without burden disproportionate to participants' perceived benefit.

### AP-P6 — Authority containment

The experiment can make its methods and interpretations useful without participants treating the Observatory's representations as official evaluations or funding recommendations.

All six hypotheses are meaningfully challengeable.

No hypothesis is supported merely because participants prefer a more polished presentation.

## 6. Experimental Method

### Phase A — Retrospective calibration

Select one existing HTEP Corpus history with enough source material to support multiple readings and enough ambiguity to challenge premature conclusions.

Prepare a small number of representations of the same underlying evidence:

**A. Source-first view**

Source records, provenance, and minimal navigation, without supplied Observatory interpretation.

**B. Structured evidence view**

A restrained organization of observations, economic distinctions, relationships, and uncertainty, each traceable to sources.

**C. Plural interpretation view**

The same evidence with multiple explicitly attributed or clearly labeled hypothetical interpretations, where the evidence genuinely permits disagreement.

The purpose is to discover whether the representations affect comprehension, traceability, perceived certainty, or deference to presented interpretations.

The same underlying source evidence must be available across conditions. No representation may silently introduce new factual claims.

A small number of voluntary readers, including at least one cold-start reader who initially receives Corpus evidence without Observatory case-memo conclusions, may participate.

Where participants encounter more than one representation, the order and possible carryover effects should be recorded. The resulting observations are exploratory, not statistically controlled estimates of causal effects.

The phase should conclude with an explicit decision about whether prospective testing is justified.

### Phase A learning gate

Proceed to Phase B only if:

- the evidence distinctions can be represented without material distortion;
- at least one participant can engage with the material independently of the Observatory's original investigator;
- the remaining uncertainties are sufficiently understood to avoid presenting the prospective exercise as an authoritative assessment;
- the expected additional learning justifies the participant burden;
- an appropriate current or forming proposal and voluntary participation are available.

If these conditions are not met, the probe may refine, redirect, or close without undertaking Phase B.

### Phase B — Prospective shadow intake

Select **one current or forming treasury proposal**.

Selection should favor a proposal for which a bounded, voluntary exercise is feasible, and whose evidence presents meaningful uncertainty or relationships worth investigating.

Preference should be given to participation by a proposer or someone directly involved in proposal formation, together with at least one reader who has a genuine proposal-interpretation role or relevant practice.

A DRep is a desirable participant but is not an exclusive or mandatory role.

The exercise should attempt to capture a minimal evidence profile, potentially including:

- what is being proposed and requested;
- material assumptions behind the estimate;
- the economic meaning of monetary amounts;
- relevant prior funding or dependencies;
- stated deliverables and anticipated evidence;
- identified uncertainty, missing information, and unresolved questions;
- sources supporting the submitted claims.

Fields should be included only where their potential usefulness justifies their burden.

The structured evidence should then be offered for independent interpretation.

Participants may identify useful distinctions, challenge assumptions, add competing explanations, question completeness, or judge the representation unhelpful.

The experiment does not ask participants to issue funding recommendations.

Where the selected proposal is actively undergoing governance, outputs must not be portrayed as an Observatory endorsement, certification, or official assessment. Participation or publication must not imply that the proposal received privileged evaluation or approval.

The exercise should conclude before expanding to additional proposals.

## 7. Intake Design Principle

The prospective intake should be governed by a two-sided usefulness test:

> **What present judgment could this information improve?**

> **What future learning could preserving it make possible?**

Neither question presumes that additional information is always beneficial.

A field should not be required merely because it was useful to an earlier historical reconstruction.

Historical missingness may suggest a prospective question, but does not by itself justify imposing that question on current proposers.

This principle is intended to constrain evidence collection, avoid retrospective schema bias, and protect the ability of novel work to remain appropriately uncertain.

## 8. Participant Conditions and Research Ethics

Participation must be voluntary and informed.

Participants should understand:

- that AP-001 is a formation experiment, not an official treasury assessment;
- what they are being asked to review or contribute;
- how their responses may be recorded and used;
- what material may become public;
- whether contributions will be attributed or anonymized;
- that they may decline questions or withdraw before their responses are incorporated into a published research record.

Only public or explicitly authorized proposal materials should be incorporated into a public Corpus record.

Unpublished or sensitive proposal information must not be collected for public release without the relevant contributor's permission.

No raw private responses or personal identifying details should be published by default. Public research records should use participant-approved quotations, aggregated observations where meaningful, or appropriately minimized summaries.

The experiment should distinguish observed behavior from investigator interpretation. If participants disagree with the investigator's characterization of their response, that disagreement should be documented.

Where participation or later disclosure could influence an active funding decision, the limitation and potential conflict must be addressed before proceeding. If it cannot be addressed proportionately, use a public, completed, or non-active proposal instead.

## 9. Observation and Interpretation

AP-001 should evaluate usefulness through observed participant experience rather than through investigator preference.

Possible observations include:

| Dimension | Inquiry |
|---|---|
| Comprehension | Can readers distinguish recorded facts, assumptions, inference, and missingness? |
| Provenance | Can material claims be traced to the evidence that supports them? |
| Relevance | Which distinctions do participants themselves consider useful, and why? |
| Plurality | Can different interpretations coexist without being collapsed into an official conclusion? |
| False comparability | Does the presentation imply equivalence where economic meaning or evidentiary support differs? |
| Anchoring | Does a presented structure or interpretation appear to influence subsequent reasoning? |
| Authority | Do readers treat the representation as an independent aid or as an Observatory judgment? |
| Prospective reusability | Would the captured assumptions and source links make later reconstruction more feasible? |
| Burden | What effort is required, and is that effort justified by participants' reported usefulness? |

The exercise should use short, open-ended prompts where practical.

Participants should be able to explain what they understood, what remained unclear, what they questioned, and what they would do differently with or without the representation.

No composite assessment score should be generated.

Findings about anchoring and authority must be qualified by the small sample and experimental design. The absence of an observed distortion is not proof that the distortion cannot occur.

## 10. Evidence Handling

The experiment should preserve enough material for its claims and methods to be inspected independently.

### Treasury Evidence Corpus

Where appropriate, reusable public evidence may include:

- original source and provenance references;
- proposal-supplied evidence that is public or approved for release;
- bounded prospective evidence records;
- documented relationships and source-backed claims;
- explicit missingness and uncertainty;
- experimental schema or vocabulary additions, if justified.

The Corpus should not become a repository for private participant research responses or Observatory judgments.

### Treasury Observatory

The probe record should retain:

- the experiment agreement and any material amendments;
- sample-selection rationale;
- the representation variants used;
- prompts and method notes;
- participant-consented research observations;
- competing interpretations and limitations;
- prospective burden log;
- phase-gate decision;
- findings and learning;
- implications for parent hypotheses.

Source evidence, analytical transformations, participant interpretations, and funding judgments must remain distinguishable.

## 11. Burden Measurement

HTEP-001 established the importance of burden but did not fulfill its original prospective task-level effort-logging commitment.

AP-001 should correct that methodological limitation without introducing excessive administration.

A lightweight prospective log should capture approximate effort for:

- preparing or adapting sources;
- producing representation variants;
- prospective intake and clarification;
- participant reading and interpretation;
- review and corrections;
- evidence preservation and documentation.

Where practical, participant-reported time and perceived effort should be distinguished from researcher preparation time.

Unrecorded effort should remain explicitly unmeasured rather than reconstructed as false precision.

The learning record should also consider likely maintenance and repeat-use costs, clearly separated from observed effort.

## 12. Reflexive Observation

The prototype itself may affect participant behavior.

AP-001 must investigate whether:

- clearer categories encourage participants to overstate certainty;
- an Observatory-authored representation appears more credible than the underlying evidence warrants;
- historical comparisons anchor expectations;
- proposer responses shift toward satisfying perceived assessment categories;
- participant interpretations become less diverse after encountering a structured view;
- apparent evidence completeness obscures important unknowns;
- burdens fall differently on proposers, readers, and maintainers.

These are not merely hazards to prevent. They are potential evidence about whether the proposed stewardship function is viable.

The Observatory must remain part of the system being observed.

## 13. Outputs

### Required

The probe should produce:

1. A retrospective calibration package using one existing Corpus history.
2. A record of the Phase A learning gate and its disposition.
3. If Phase B is activated, one bounded prospective intake and representation package.
4. Participant-judged usefulness and interpretation observations, with limitations.
5. An explicit record of observed or suspected anchoring, false certainty, and authority effects.
6. A prospective burden record.
7. A learning and closure record with implications for H03, H04, H07, H10, and relevant cross-cutting hypotheses.

### Optional, only if justified by observed need

- Minimal reusable intake guidance.
- Small evidence-schema or vocabulary refinements.
- A reusable representation template.
- Recommendations for a future prospective evidence-capture probe.

The probe is not required to produce a working application, comprehensive template library, or institutional proposal intake specification.

## 14. Closure Conditions

AP-001 may close when:

- Phase A has been conducted and interpreted, or a justified early stop is recorded;
- the Phase B activation decision has been documented;
- if Phase B proceeds, the single prospective exercise has been completed or stopped for a documented reason;
- research materials and public evidence have been retained at an appropriate level of fidelity;
- participants' interpretations are not silently replaced by investigator conclusions;
- uncertainty, disagreement, and methodological limitations remain visible;
- prospective burden observations are available, with any measurement gaps declared;
- material authority or anchoring risks have been examined;
- parent hypotheses receive relevant learning updates;
- the next formation path is recommended with a supporting rationale.

Completion does not require demonstrating improved decision quality or producing a preferred assessment format.

A finding that the representations are unnecessary, misleading, burdensome, or unusable is an acceptable probe result.

## 15. Possible Outcomes

**Continue**

The evidence and participant observations justify additional, separately authorized experiments in prospective evidence intake or representation.

**Refine**

Some benefit is apparent, but evidence structures, representation methods, participant interactions, or burden require adjustment.

**Redirect**

The primary limitation appears to lie elsewhere—for example, source availability, prospective capture, external cost comparators, bottom-up estimation, or access to relevant domain expertise.

**Stop**

The learning or usefulness demonstrated does not justify further development of this particular approach.

No outcome automatically authorizes a production service, institutional assessment role, or expanded collection program.

## 16. Formation Learning Questions

The closure should explicitly answer:

1. Did the experiment improve our understanding of how evidence representation affects participant interpretation?
2. Did historical evidence generate useful distinctions for prospective proposal creation or deliberation?
3. Did prospective capture appear capable of preserving evidence that would otherwise be difficult to reconstruct?
4. Did participants retain independent judgment, or did the representation encourage deference?
5. What was the burden, who bore it, and what usefulness justified it?
6. What changed in our understanding of the Observatory's appropriate stewardship domain?
7. What should be investigated next, and what should be left alone?

The distinction between **observed results**, **participant reports**, and **future hypotheses** must remain explicit.

## 17. Working Synthesis

HTEP-001 explored whether treasury history could become reusable shared learning.

AP-001 explores whether that learning can improve how participants create, interpret, and preserve evidence during contemporary treasury activity.

These are related but different inquiries.

The Observatory should not become an institution primarily devoted to reconstructing and judging past funding decisions. Nor should it become a centralized authority over future ones.

Its potential contribution is to help participants generate more intelligible evidence, reason from it independently, preserve their distinctions and disagreements, and carry useful learning forward.

> **The purpose of better historical memory is not to perfect the judgment of the past. It is to improve the conditions under which the present learns and the future can learn again.**

**Status remains Proposed until this agreement is reviewed and merged.**
