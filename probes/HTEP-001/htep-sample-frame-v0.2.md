## 1. Purpose of this revision

Carry Phase 1 into an intentionally small second phase. The sample is **purposive methodological sampling**, not a representative survey or an obligation to populate a database of approximately 30 cases. Its unit is a *bounded funding history* when useful, not necessarily a proposal, transaction, organization, or one funding action.

**Sampling driver:** Discover whether evidence about historically diverse treasury funding can be recovered, related, interpreted for costs and delivery, and preserved for other investigators at a burden proportionate to what is learned—without silently erasing differences in the source histories.

This revision is an **operational conduct record** of HTEP-001, not a change to its authorized domain, hypotheses, or closure conditions. Phase 1 case memos and underlying source evidence remain independently referencable; this document does not supersede their observations.

### Five learning claims carried forward

1. **The topology is heterogeneous.** Governance actions, grant awards, revisions, funding histories, administrative judgments, payment events and delivery claims are different things; there is no natural single row to which all can be safely reduced.
2. **Money requires semantics.** A requested sum, authorized withdrawal, estimated work cost, contingency, reported disbursement and returned amount cannot be treated as interchangeable just because they share a denomination.
3. **Relationships are sourced assertions.** Explicit revision/continuation links are useful; shared people, technical domains and organizations are not sufficient by themselves to demonstrate causal or funding continuity.
4. **More records do not necessarily mean stronger verification.** Mature milestone reviews document institutional decisions more clearly; an earlier proposal may offer more interpretable planned labor costs. Neither gives independently verified realized project costs by default.
5. **Missingness is observed evidence.** Broken links, source-version changes, apparent display inconsistencies, unavailable rationale and incomplete chain-of-funds records should remain visible, not be repaired by interpretation.

### Checkpoint evaluation of HTEP local hypotheses

| Hypothesis | Phase 1 standing | Phase 2 implication |
|---|---|---|
| **P1 — Reusable evidence** | Promising; original source preservation uneven | Test whether a new investigator can trace key claims to stable, obtainable artifacts |
| **P2 — Minimal normalization** | Promising as a *light observational vocabulary*, not a universal schema | Reuse distinctions only where the new case requires them; avoid ontology expansion by default |
| **P3 — Longitudinal learning** | Strong conceptual benefit from continuations, revisions, dated reporting and milestone sequences | Challenge with older, partial or multi-step funding chains |

## 3. A contextual observation Phase 1 made newly salient: sustaining contributors

Early Catalyst's aspirations included not just producing infrastructure or discrete deliverables, but enabling contributors and contributor organizations to develop and persist. The S3 investigation showed why shared contributors or neighboring projects do not establish a *grant continuation*, yet such relationships might still matter historically.

**TxPipe as a candidate investigation lens, not a verified conclusion:** Pallas, Dolos, Demeter and related work may offer a way to observe how successive funded efforts related to the growth and sustained productive capacity of a contributor laboratory. This hypothesis of connection requires source-by-source reconstruction; neither organizational persistence nor ecosystem impact is inferred from common attribution or repeated awards.

**Why it belongs within cost verification:** A defensible account of what treasury spending purchased may include technical artifacts, maintenance, retained expertise, contributor capacity, coordination and future capability. Those phenomena do not replace disciplined fiscal observation; they help clarify *which work and which results the costs could meaningfully be compared against*. Cost evidence can be accurate but still misleading when detached from its effective context.

**Observation rule:** Where directly relevant and supported, note (a) contributor/organization identity as attested, (b) apparent continuity of people, practices or capabilities, (c) funding or delivery links actually evidenced, and (d) claims of capability development and their evidentiary status. Preserve rival explanations and avoid converting contributor growth into a single monetary return-on-investment estimate.

**Restraint:** This is a **contextual lens**, not a sixth local hypothesis, an obligation to map contributor networks, a new mandatory schema, or authority to assert that treasury funding caused later success. Consider activating a TxPipe/Pallas history *only if* Phase 2 exposes a consequential question that it can distinguish.

### Common intake practice

For each activated case, record only what is observed and useful:

1. **Sources:** stable identifiers, responsible publisher, access/retrieval date, original source and preservation state; distinguish source statements from investigator interpretation.
2. **Observed events:** funding request, selection, authorization, withdrawal, allocation, distribution, return, reported expenditure, report, review or outcome **only where attested**; differentiate `absent`, `not applicable`, `not checked`, `unavailable` and `conflicting`.
3. **Relationships:** subject, object, relationship claim, claimant/source, evidence and verification status; no automatic permanent initiative or contributor identifier.
4. **Amounts and costs:** currency, amount, original time/reference, nature of amount, estimated/reported/verified status, scope and denominator when available; no forced exchange-rate or cross-era adjustment.
5. **Burden:** rough minutes or short effort band for discovery, retrieval, extraction and verification; number of significant dead ends; highest-friction source; what a repeat investigator would need. **Do not retroactively invent Phase 1 effort.**
6. **Bounded memo:** strongest recoverable claim, consequential uncertainty, what this case challenges from Phase 1, new distinctions genuinely required, and close/reopen decision.

These are optional observational structures, **not** requirements to introduce tables for each type or collect every field.

## 5. Phase 2 learning checkpoint

**Trigger:** At **S5 closure and the next selection gate**, make a small check: did the early-history boundary expose a genuinely new obstacle or evidence regime, and is S6 still the best use of attention? A fuller **Phase 2 checkpoint** follows **no later than S7, or earlier if incremental learning becomes low**. The checkpoint is based on learning, not calendar time or completing three cases.

Address:

- **P1 — Traceability:** Can a reader independently recover a consequential claim, and what important sources are missing or mutable?
- **P2 — Representability:** Which Phase 1 distinctions held up; what genuinely new concepts were indispensable; where did a universal schema invite category errors?
- **P3 — History and relationship:** Did pre-milestone evidence, delegated authority or contributor-capacity context change what is knowable over time? Which relationships are asserted, demonstrated, merely plausible or contradicted?
- **P4 — Cost usefulness:** Did any actual candidate comparator emerge with matching economic meaning, cost basis, scope, time and unit? If not, what is the precise evidence limitation?
- **P5 — Burden:** How much effort was consumed per decision-relevant assertion? Which retrieval paths were abandoned, and would a fifth similar intake teach something new?

**Decision outputs:** `continue`, `refine`, `redirect`, or `stop`, with a short explanation of sufficiency, uncertainty and expected marginal learning. A *negative finding* (e.g., early evidence irrecoverable or delegation untraceable) is legitimate learning. A checkpoint may reframe later sampling without silently amending the active probe.

## 6. Stopping discipline: what Phase 1 taught about stopping

Stopping is not a number or the absence of curiosity. It is an explicit, inspectable judgment that further effort is unlikely to change a consequential decision **at a proportionate burden**.

Phase 1 strengthened the discipline in six ways:

1. **A completed intake is not a completed history.** S1–S4 retain worthwhile open questions; closure means those questions did not need resolution to answer *this* test.
2. **New evidence need not justify new scope.** Eternl's Catalyst history and S3's contributor network were useful leads, but each could be preserved without launching a second investigation inside the first.
3. **Not every discrepancy demands reconciliation now.** S4's zero-allocation warning was captured rather than silently corrected or escalated into a financial audit.
4. **Negative and partial results count.** Unretrievable documents, unverifiable expenditure and provisional institutional statements are findings with source-specific limits, not failures to make a database complete.
5. **The stopping rule is also about authority.** Declining to overclaim from an approval, budget estimate or completion label is part of the Observatory's epistemic legitimacy.
6. **Proportionality must be made more observable.** Phase 1 tracked boundedness qualitatively but not effort consistently. Phase 2's small effort record is a methodological correction, not a demand for timesheet precision.

**Per-case stop:** close when the discriminating question has a sourced answer or a bounded account of why it cannot presently be answered, the main claims have qualifiers, and further verification would be a distinct decision.

**Phase stop:** close Phase 2 when additional candidate histories are predicted to repeat already observed uncertainty or add less consequential learning than their retrieval burden. It is permissible to stop after S5 or S6, to substitute a more discriminating case, or to proceed to the HTEP check/learn/close path without reaching the originally imagined ~30 histories.

**Reopen only if:** a new source materially changes a funding relationship, a cost interpretation, a hypothesis judgment, or the next sampling decision. Curiosity, completeness, publicity and an appealing side investigation are not sufficient reopening conditions.

## 7. Relationship to the parent probe and repositories

- **HTEP-001 remains active, in `conduct`.** This is a learning and sampling decision within its existing driver and domain. Do not mark the probe completed because Phase 1 has closed.
- **Observatory repo:** this sample frame, selection decisions, bounded intakes, learning checkpoints, and interpretations including the contributor-capacity observation.
- **Evidence Corpus repo:** faithful source artifacts, retrieval and transformation provenance, and minimally useful records that can be reused without adopting the Observatory's interpretation. Do not migrate unverified case judgments into a canonical evidence layer.
- **No new administrative structure by default.** Use the existing probe path and linked PR/issue process; prefer a replacement/current sample-frame document with a clear version history to a proliferation of governance artifacts.

### HTEP completion is still a separate decision

The probe's original approximately 30-case ambition remains a **revisable forecast**, not an amended mandatory target. The eventual `check → learn → close` decision must ask whether sufficient contrasting evidence has been gathered to evaluate its hypotheses and inform the Observatory's formation choices. The case-study method should not become a self-justifying production line.

## 8. Forecast after Phase 2 — not a preauthorization

Depending on what S5–S7 change, subsequent choices may include:

- **Stop and synthesize:** If Phase 2 mostly confirms the five Phase 1 distinctions and deeper cost verification remains systematically uneconomic, turn to HTEP synthesis rather than pursue nominal sample size.
- **Focused comparability test:** If a defensible reference class emerges (e.g., planned labor units for technically related work), perform a small matched test of comparability rather than broaden coverage indiscriminately.
- **Focused delegated-funding chain:** If S6 demonstrates an observable intermediary-to-recipient edge, test one additional chain only if it challenges the model; distinguish grant, investment and allocation regimes.
- **Contributor-capacity history:** If a Pallas/TxPipe case suggests observable capability development, test whether that context changes a cost interpretation, without attempting a comprehensive network-impact or attribution study.
- **Targeted evidence-recovery test:** If the earliest sources prove irrecoverable, decide whether a bounded alternate archive is worth checking—or explicitly record the historical limit.

**Post-Phase 2 gate:** Select at most one or two *new discriminating questions* before authorizing further cohorts. Revisit the ~30-case forecast only if new questions justify additional coverage; the number itself must never provide the justification.
