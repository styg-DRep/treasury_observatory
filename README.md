# Treasury Observatory

The **Treasury Observatory** is a formation-stage effort to explore how a decentralized public funding commons can build durable, shared, contestable capacity to learn from its own activity.

It is not a treasury office, rating agency, grant committee, or centralized evaluator.

Its purpose is to improve the conditions under which participants can observe, compare, interpret, and learn from treasury activity while preserving plural judgment and decentralized decision-making.

## Why This Exists

Public treasury activity generates substantial evidence about costs, estimates, delivery, outcomes, and the behavior of the funding system.

That evidence is often distributed across proposals, governance records, milestone reports, audits, funding histories, transactions, and later outcomes.

As a result, participants may repeatedly face similar stewardship questions without a durable shared capacity to carry learning forward across funding decisions and governance cycles.

The Treasury Observatory explores whether that capacity can be stewarded as a commons function.

## Working Driver

> Public treasury activity generates substantial evidence about costs, estimates, delivery, outcomes, and the behavior of the funding system, but that evidence remains distributed and difficult to compare or carry forward across funding decisions and governance cycles.
>
> Consequently, participants repeatedly make stewardship decisions without a durable shared capacity to learn from prior activity, recognize emerging systemic patterns, or distinguish warranted uncertainty from avoidable information asymmetry.
>
> Strengthening that capacity could help the funding commons adapt its practices while preserving plural judgment and decentralized decision-making.

## Stewardship Domain

A **stewardship domain** is a field of conditions, resources, relationships, and practices for which participants intentionally coordinate care, learning, and responsibility without claiming exclusive ownership or centralized control.

For the Treasury Observatory, that domain is:

> **the shared capacity to observe, preserve, compare, interpret, and learn from evidence about the behavior of a decentralized public funding commons.**

This includes attention to:

- evidence structures;
- historical and longitudinal memory;
- methods of comparison and estimation;
- uncertainty;
- analytical provenance;
- competing interpretations;
- developmental inquiries;
- learning generated through treasury activity;
- the effects of the Observatory itself.

The Observatory does not own this domain.

## Formation Approach

This repository is currently part of a **Formation Inquiry**:

> **Can a Treasury Observatory materially improve stewardship of a decentralized public funding commons, and what is the smallest institutional form capable of doing so without displacing plural judgment or creating unnecessary authority?**

Formation work follows a developmental rather than prescriptive approach.

The current sequence is roughly:

```text
Driver
  ↓
Concept Charter
  ↓
Design Principles
  ↓
Hypothesis Registry
  ↓
Formation Pathmap
  ↓
Probes / Inquiries
  ↓
Learning
  ↺
```

This is a **pathmap**, not a roadmap.

The next step should be chosen based on what consequential uncertainty is most useful to reduce, not because it was precommitted as a deliverable.

## Core Design Principles

The current design principles include:

- steward the conditions for judgment, not judgment itself;
- keep observation, interpretation, and judgment distinguishable;
- make uncertainty visible;
- centralize memory more readily than authority;
- keep authority bounded, explicit, and temporary where possible;
- allow shared evidence without requiring shared interpretation;
- keep methods inspectable, versioned, and contestable;
- let credibility emerge empirically and contextually;
- use history as evidence rather than as an automatic norm;
- preserve the possibility of novelty;
- prefer reversible experiments to premature institutionalization;
- let structure follow recurring need;
- keep participation costs proportionate;
- treat the Observatory as part of the system it observes;
- preserve reflexivity as a first-class function;
- optimize for learning across time;
- follow consequential uncertainty;
- favor commons capacity over institutional dependency;
- preserve separation between cost, delivery, and public value;
- hold the domain, not the territory.

The full principles live in the formation documents.

## Distributed Developmental Evaluation

The Observatory treats evaluation as a distributed function rather than as the exclusive role of a designated evaluator.

The working developmental loop is:

```text
observation
    ↓
interpretation
    ↓
hypothesis
    ↓
experiment or adaptation
    ↓
further observation
```

Different participants may contribute at different points in the loop.

The Observatory seeks to provide shared memory, visible provenance, explicit methods, and inquiry practices that make this distributed function more coherent.

## Current Formation Path

The current Formation Pathmap groups uncertainty into four broad territories:

1. **Can shared evidence produce useful learning?**
2. **Can analysis improve judgment without becoming authority?**
3. **Can evaluation remain distributed?**
4. **Can the Observatory remain a healthy commons institution over time?**

The current next edge is the:

**Historical Treasury Evidence Probe**

This probe will test whether a bounded sample of historical treasury activity can be transformed into reusable, provenance-preserving evidence that supports longitudinal sensemaking and cost inquiry.

## How Probes Work

A probe is a bounded formation experiment.

The minimum working pattern is:

```text
Establish
   ↓
Activate
   ↓
Conduct
   ↓
Check
   ↓
Learn
   ↓
Close
```

A probe is proposed through a pull request.

Review should ask whether the probe:

- responds to the Formation Pathmap;
- has a bounded domain;
- makes relevant hypotheses explicit;
- aligns with current Design Principles;
- appears proportionate to the learning sought.

Once merged, the probe becomes active.

At closure, results are checked for evidence support, epistemic integrity, explicit limitations, burden, and learning.

Review is not intended to enforce agreement with an interpretation.

## Relationship to the Treasury Evidence Corpus

The Treasury Observatory and the **Treasury Evidence Corpus** are intentionally separate stewardship objects.

The Observatory holds:

- questions;
- methods;
- hypotheses;
- inquiries;
- interpretations;
- learning.

The Treasury Evidence Corpus holds:

- source records;
- provenance;
- normalized evidence objects;
- schemas;
- reusable datasets.

The Observatory may use the Corpus, but it should not own the underlying evidence commons conceptually.

This separation helps preserve the distinction between evidence and interpretation.

## Contribution

- reviews of active work.

A contribution does not need to agree with existing conclusions.

## Working Principles

> **Shared memory, plural sensemaking, temporary authority.**

> **Centralize what improves learning; distribute what requires judgment.**

> **Follow consequential uncertainty rather than precommitted deliverables.**

> **The path is not a promise about where the Observatory will go. It is a visible account of why the next step appears worth taking.**
